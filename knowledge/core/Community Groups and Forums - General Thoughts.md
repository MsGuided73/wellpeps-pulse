WellPeps Community Groups and Forums

**Community marketing is becoming more valuable as consumers trust traditional**

advertising less. At the same time, it carries some of the highest compliance and reputational risks in healthcare.

**I actually think this could become one of WellPeps' competitive advantages.**

Many big, corporate telehealth companies either avoid engaging with communities altogether or rely on generic marketing responses. A community team that provides transparent, educational, and genuinely helpful participation can build trust over time while staying within appropriate compliance boundaries., I see treat community engagement as a core part of the brand rather than just another marketing channel.

## General Community Engagement Policy

We will build an official Community Engagement Policy that applies to employees, contractors, influencers, and ambassadors. It will include:

- Approved disclosure language

- What can and cannot be discussed

- How to answer common questions

- Examples of compliant vs. non-compliant responses

- Escalation procedures for medical questions

- Privacy and HIPAA reminders

- FTC endorsement requirements

- Platform-specific guidance (Facebook, Reddit, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn)

### First Principle

**Never try to disguise marketing as organic discussion.**

If a WellPeps employee pretends to be "just another person" recommending WellPeps, or if influencers fail to disclose their relationship, that can create FTC issues and seriously damage trust if discovered.

Transparency and authenticity is our friend.

# Option 1 — Identified Employee

This is probably my favorite approach.

For example:

"Hi, I'm Derek. I help with wellness education at WellPeps. We just published a blog article in our Wellness Learning Center on that topic. Here’s a link. Hope it helps.”

or

"I'm part of the WellPeps team."

That disclosure is important.

Then the team members contributes genuinely useful information.

Examples:

- discussing telehealth generally

- linking to educational articles - Wellness Learning Center

- answering process questions

Not: "Come buy our GLP-1."

# Option 1 — WellPeps Official Account

I think this should be our second primary approach.

Examples:

- WellPeps Facebook Page

- WellPeps Reddit account

- WellPeps X account

- WellPeps LinkedIn

The account can answer questions such as:

"What is the difference between semaglutide and tirzepatide?"

or

"How does telehealth work?"

without telling someone which medication they should take.

Benefits:

- transparent

- builds authority

- low legal risk

- establishes trust

The tone should be educational, not promotional.

# Option 3 — Physician or Provider

If licensed clinicians like Dr. Yirga ever participate, they should do so only under carefully defined guidelines. They can contribute valuable educational content, but they should avoid creating a physician-patient relationship in a public forum or giving individualized medical advice. In my opinion, it’s too risky.

# Option 4 — Influencers and Brand Reps

Influencers should generally direct people to WellPeps rather than answering medical questions.

Good Example: | "I had a good experience with WellPeps."

Bad Example: | "You should definitely take tirzepatide."

# Option 5 — Anonymous Accounts

I would avoid this entirely.

Creating anonymous or fake grassroots accounts to praise WellPeps or steer conversations is risky from both a compliance and reputational standpoint. If discovered, it can permanently undermine credibility.

# Thoughts about Reddit

Reddit is different. People there often dislike obvious marketing.

I would not join Reddit just to sell.

Instead: Answer questions. Be helpful.

Only mention WellPeps when it directly answers the question and your connection is disclosed.

For example:

User:

"Anyone know how online GLP-1 clinics work?"

Appropriate response:

"I work with WellPeps. In general, telehealth clinics operate by having a licensed provider review your health history before deciding whether treatment is appropriate. Regardless of the company, it's important that the evaluation is conducted by a licensed clinician."

Notice:

The answer helps everyone. It doesn't feel like an advertisement.

# Facebook Groups

Facebook tends to be more accepting of identified business participation, but group rules vary widely.

We should::

- Ask administrators for permission if the group limits promotional activity.

- Participate regularly rather than appearing only when promoting WellPeps.

- Contribute educational content and answer general questions.

- Share blog posts only when they genuinely answer a question or add value.

That helps establish credibility over time.

- WellPeps Healthy Aging Community

Notice: Not "Buy GLP-1."

Instead: Lifestyle. Nutrition. Exercise. Recipes. Motivation. Medical education.

That community becomes an owned audience rather than relying entirely on third-party platforms.

# The Standard: the 80/20 Rule

I would aim for roughly:

**80% Education**

Examples:

- FAQs

- myths vs. facts

- nutrition

- sleep

- exercise

- telehealth process

- medication education

- side effect management (in general terms)

**20% Promotion**

Examples:

- free assessment

- new services

- blog articles

- webinars

- educational videos

Most healthcare brands reverse this ratio and end up sounding like advertisers.

# Compliance Considerations

Here are the rules for everyone representing WellPeps:

**1: Always disclose affiliation**

Never hide that you work with or represent WellPeps.

**2: Never give individualized medical advice**

Avoid responses like:

"Based on what you described, you should take…"

Instead:

"That's something a licensed provider would need to evaluate."

**3. Don't discuss someone's medical history publicly**

If someone starts sharing symptoms or medications:

"For your privacy, it's best not to discuss personal medical details here. If you'd like to learn about our process, we're happy to explain how evaluations work."

**4: Avoid direct solicitation**

Don't jump into discussions just to promote WellPeps. Instead, answer questions honestly and let the value of the information speak for itself.
