**Community Engagement – Why and when to engage.**

**The engagement-selection policy starts with the business goal and defines which**

conversations are worth entering. Relevance to a medication alone is too broad.

Our motivatio: gain new customers and build awareness and authority by making WellPeps a useful, trustworthy, approachable presence. Our agent should understand that commercial purpose without turning each reply into a sales pitch.

I would give it the following framework.

**The goal**

Identify conversations where a WellPeps representative can provide useful information, address an expressed unmet need, or help someone exploring relevant care options.

Prefer conversations that can build trust with prospective patients and the wider audience. A useful educational answer may create value even when the original poster never becomes a customer.

**The selection rule**

Recommend responding only when all three conditions are met:

1. There is an opening: a genuine question, request for education, expressed unmet need, or relevant request for options. 2. WellPeps can contribute: approved information, a relevant educational resource, or a verified service capability addresses that need. 3. Participation makes sense: the community permits it, the contribution adds something useful, and it will not feel intrusive or opportunistic.

Then apply the existing safety, privacy and clinical rules.

**Two distinctions matter for your exclusions.**

For an existing patient of another provider, the agent should distinguish:

- “I’m with Ro and things are going well.” → Skip.

- “I’m with Ro, unhappy with the support, and looking elsewhere.” → Evaluate the

**unmet need and approved WellPeps fit.**

Our default can remain “skip satisfied-provider conversations.” The exception is an explicit educational question where WellPeps adds clear value.

For price conversations:

- “Who can beat $99?” → Usually skip.

- “Why do these prices differ, and what should I check?” → Educational opportunity.

- “What does WellPeps charge?” → Answer directly with approved facts.

**The agent should explain why it surfaced each post**

Each recommendation should include:

- Opening: What is the person actually asking for?

- Contribution: What useful answer can we provide?

- Strategic value: Education, awareness, expressed treatment interest, provider alternatives or direct WellPeps interest.

- Evidence: Which approved information supports the answer?

- Resource fit: Relevant eBook, relevant webpage or none.

- Decision: Respond, skip, hold or route—with a brief reason.

This prevents “mentions semaglutide” from becoming the entire justification.

**The resource comes after the engagement decision**

The agent should first decide whether WellPeps belongs in the conversation. Then decide whether a link helps.

An eBook is appropriate when it meaningfully expands on the question. A program page fits an explicit request for service information. Neither should be forced into an otherwise useful reply.

A concise core instruction would be:

**Find conversations where WellPeps can earn trust by answering a relevant question or**

**addressing an expressed unmet need. Prioritize useful education, people exploring**

appropriate care options, relevant requests for alternatives, and direct WellPeps questions.

**Usually skip satisfied-provider conversations, cheapest-price requests, unrelated topics,**

**adequately answered threads and posts without an opening. Do not invent relevance.**

**Explain the value of responding before drafting. Select a resource only when it helps.**

**Apply permission, safety, privacy and clinical rules before any commercial prioritization.**

I would also have the agent collect recurring skipped questions that lack approved answers. Those are useful signals for future eBooks, website content and Knowledge Base updates—even when no public response is appropriate.
