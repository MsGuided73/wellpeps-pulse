Community Engagement – Offering Links For most message-board conversations, I’d lead with a useful answer and offer the relevant eBook. However, use a program or product page when the person is asking about providers, WellPeps, pricing or how to get treatment.

The link should follow the person’s intent:

| What the person is asking | Best next step |
|---|---|
| “What’s the difference between these treatments?” | Answer generally; offer the relevant eBook if it explains the differences. |
| “What should I know before choosing a provider?” | Share useful selection criteria; offer a guide covering those questions. |
| “Anyone recommend another provider?” | Briefly disclose your affiliation and explain relevant approved WellPeps facts; a program page or Why WellPeps may fit |
| “What does WellPeps offer or charge?” | Answer directly using current approved information; link to the relevant program page. |
| “How do I get started with WellPeps?” | Answer and provide the approved screening or program link, subject to current posting rules. |
| “I feel unwell—should I change medication?” | Provider/safety direction; no eBook or product pitch. |

The transition to any eBook landing page or website product page or website home page should explain why the resource helps, rather than simply announce that WellPeps has something to offer.

For an eBook, use this sequence: answer → identify what the guide explains → optional link.

For example, after answering a general treatment question:

“I work with WellPeps on patient education. Our free GLP-1 eBook goes into those differences and the questions to discuss with a provider. Here’s the link to get the free eBook : [approved guide link].”

Use that wording only if the guide actually covers those subjects. The helpful answer should appear before the offer, so the person gets value without downloading anything.

For a program page, where possible, answer their specific question in the comment too. Don’t make them click just to discover a price or basic service detail you could have stated.

I wouldn’t force everyone through the eBook first. Someone seeking education may appreciate a guide; someone asking what WellPeps costs probably wants the program page. An email-gated download would add an unnecessary step for that second person.

For the AI, I’d make the rule:

Choose one resource that answers the person’s current question. Offer the eBook for deeper understanding; offer the program page for explicitly requested service information. If neither adds value, include no link.

All of this remains subject to verified community permission and our current approved link rules—including the pre-LegitScript restrictions we discussed. A relevant topic alone isn’t an invitation to promote.
