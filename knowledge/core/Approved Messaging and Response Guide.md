WellPeps

*Approved Messaging & Response Guide*

Day-to-Day Reference for Community Engagement, Social Media, Influencers and Brand Ambassadors

Version 1.0 | August 2026 | Internal Use

# 1. Purpose of This Guide

This guide provides approved response patterns and messaging guardrails for authorized WellPeps representatives. It is intended to be the practical reference kept open during day-to-day community engagement.

It should be used with the WellPeps Community Engagement Policy, Community Engagement Operations & Training Manual, and Community Engagement Compliance Training.

> How to Use It Find the type of question or situation, use the approved response pattern, adapt ordinary conversational wording only as needed, and preserve the meaning, disclosures, claims, privacy boundaries and escalation requirements.

Important This Version 1.0 is grounded in the WellPeps community-engagement materials supplied for this project. Where those materials do not establish a specific company fact, price, support channel, clinical claim, escalation contact or approved link, this guide does not invent one. Those items are marked for WellPeps to finalize.

# 2. Universal Messaging Rules

| APPROVED / DO | DO NOT |
|---|---|
| • Answer the actual question. • Be transparent about WellPeps affiliation. • Educate before promoting. • Use current approved company information. • Keep responses concise and human. • Protect privacy. • Refer individualized treatment decisions to licensed providers. • Escalate when required. | • Diagnose or prescribe. • Recommend a medication or dose to an individual. • Interpret lab results. • Guarantee results. • Say everyone qualifies. • Make unsupported safety, efficacy or comparative claims. • Argue with users. • Hide a WellPeps relationship. |

# 3. Approved Voice and Tone

## WellPeps Should Sound

- Knowledgeable
- Helpful
- Calm
- Respectful
- Transparent
- Educational
- Professional but conversational
- Patient-centered without pretending to provide clinical care

## WellPeps Should Not Sound

- Aggressive or sales-heavy
- Defensive
- Sarcastic or combative
- Overly clinical when speaking as a non-clinical representative
- Certain about an individual's medical outcome
- Dismissive of other healthcare professionals or competitors
- Scripted to the point of sounding robotic

> Writing Standard A good WellPeps response usually does four things: answers what can be answered, states the boundary when necessary, gives a useful next step, and stops before becoming individualized medical advice.

# 5. Core WellPeps Process Messaging

The supplied community-engagement materials support the following high-level process messaging.

## How Does WellPeps Work?

| Approved response | “WellPeps uses an online telehealth process. You complete a health assessment, and a licensed healthcare provider reviews your information to determine whether treatment is medically appropriate. If treatment is prescribed, ongoing support is part of the process.” |
|---|---|

| Approved response | “No. Only a licensed healthcare provider can determine whether treatment is medically appropriate after reviewing the individual’s health information.” |

| Approved pattern | “Treatment decisions are made by licensed healthcare providers through the WellPeps telehealth process. I can explain the process and direct you to the appropriate approved next step.” |
|---|---|

# 6. General Medical and Treatment Questions

## General Question: What Is the Difference Between Two Treatments?

| Approved pattern | “There are general differences between the treatment options, and I’m happy to share approved educational information about them. Which one is appropriate for a particular person depends on their medical history and requires evaluation by a licensed healthcare provider.” |
|---|---|

## Which Medication Should I Take?

| Approved response | “Only a licensed healthcare provider can determine the most appropriate treatment based on your individual medical history. I can provide general educational information, but I can’t recommend a medication for you personally.” |
|---|---|

## Which Dose Should I Take?

| Approved response | “Dosing decisions need to be made by the prescribing healthcare provider. I can’t recommend a dose through social media or community messaging.” |
|---|---|

## Should I Stop or Change My Medication?

| Approved response | “I can’t advise you to start, stop or change a medication. Please contact your prescribing healthcare provider through the appropriate care channel.” |
|---|---|

## Can You Interpret My Labs?

| Approved response | “I can’t interpret laboratory results. A licensed healthcare provider should review them with you through the appropriate care channel.” |
|---|---|

# 7. Results, Expectations and Safety Questions

## How Much Weight Will I Lose?

| Approved response | “Results vary by individual, and no specific amount of weight loss can be guaranteed.” |
|---|---|

## Will This Restore My Hair?

| Approved response | “Individual results vary, and hair-restoration results can’t be guaranteed. A licensed provider can determine whether a treatment may be appropriate after evaluating your individual circumstances.” |
|---|---|

## Will This Improve My Sexual Health?

| Approved response | “Results vary by individual, and no specific outcome can be guaranteed. Treatment decisions require an individualized evaluation by a licensed healthcare provider.” |
|---|---|

## Is This Treatment Safe?

| Approved response | “All medications can have potential risks and benefits. Whether a treatment is appropriate for a particular person requires evaluation by a licensed healthcare provider.” |
|---|---|

## Does This Always Work?

| Approved response | “No treatment outcome can be guaranteed. Individual response can vary.” |
|---|---|

## Prohibited Results/Safety Language

- “You’ll lose 30 pounds.”
- “This will definitely work.”
- “It’s completely safe.”
- “There are no side effects.”
- “Everyone qualifies.”
- “This will fix your problem.”

# 21. Misinformation Correction Formula

Use this four-part structure:

- Disclose affiliation if relevant.
- Acknowledge without attacking: “Just to clarify...”
- State the approved factual correction.
- Provide an approved source/link if useful.

| Example | “I work with WellPeps. Just to clarify, treatment isn’t automatically provided after someone completes an assessment. A licensed healthcare provider reviews the individual’s information and determines whether treatment is medically appropriate.” |
|---|---|

# 22. When to Stop Responding

- The person repeatedly demands individualized medical advice after the boundary has been explained.
- The conversation becomes abusive or threatening.
- The exchange is turning into an argument.
- A moderator asks the representative to stop.
- The issue has been escalated and further public discussion could interfere with resolution.
- The user continues posting private medical information.
- You no longer have approved information sufficient to answer accurately.

| Graceful close | “I don’t want to speculate or give you information that may not be appropriate for your individual situation. The best next step is to speak with a licensed healthcare provider through the appropriate care channel.” |
|---|---|

# 26. Words and Phrases to Avoid

- Guaranteed
- Guaranteed results
- Everyone qualifies
- Completely safe
- No risk
- No side effects
- Best treatment for you
- You should take...
- You need...
- Stop taking...
- This is definitely caused by...
- This will fix...
- Better than your doctor's treatment
- Better/safer/more effective than [competitor] unless specifically approved

## Preferred Alternatives

- “Results vary by individual.”
- “No specific outcome can be guaranteed.”
- “A licensed healthcare provider can determine whether treatment is appropriate.”
- “I can provide general educational information.”
- “I don’t want to speculate.”
- “I can explain the WellPeps process.”
- “For your privacy, please don’t post personal medical information here.”
