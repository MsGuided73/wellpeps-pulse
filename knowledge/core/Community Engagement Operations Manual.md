WellPeps

*Community Engagement Operations & Training Manual*

Comprehensive Operating Guide for Employees, Contractors, Agencies, Influencers, Brand Ambassadors and Authorized Representatives

Version 2.0 | August 2026 | Internal Use

# 1. Purpose and Operating Standard

This manual converts the WellPeps Community Engagement Policy into day-to-day operating procedures. It is designed to help authorized representatives know where and how to participate, what they may say, what they must not say, when to disclose affiliation, when to escalate, and how to document activity.

The objective is to make WellPeps a trusted educational resource in wellness and telehealth communities while protecting consumers, patient privacy, the WellPeps brand, and regulatory compliance.

> Core Standard Educate before you promote. Be transparent about affiliation. Never provide individualized medical advice. Protect privacy. When uncertain, do not guess—escalate.

## 1.1 Relationship to Other WellPeps Documents

- This manual should be used together with the WellPeps Community Engagement Policy, Marketing Compliance Manual, Brand Standards, approved messaging, and any current program-specific guidance.
- The Community Engagement Policy establishes the governing rules. This manual explains how authorized representatives should perform the work.
- If this manual conflicts with a later-approved compliance, legal, clinical, privacy, or brand directive, the later-approved directive controls.

## 1.2 Who Must Follow This Manual

- Employees and MSO personnel
- Contractors and community engagement representatives
- Marketing and public relations agencies
- Community moderators
- Influencers and Brand Ambassadors
- Affiliate partners
- Other persons specifically authorized to communicate publicly for WellPeps

Licensed clinicians require additional clinical guidance and should not rely on this manual alone when participating in public discussions.

# 2. Community Engagement Philosophy

Community engagement is not disguised advertising. WellPeps should earn attention by being useful. Representatives should contribute information that helps a community understand wellness topics, telehealth, treatment categories, and the WellPeps process without turning every interaction into a sales pitch.

## 2.1 The 80/20 Operating Principle

As a practical planning guideline, community activity should generally favor education over promotion.

- Approximately 80% educational/value-oriented participation: FAQs, wellness education, myths and facts, telehealth process, nutrition, exercise, sleep, general treatment-category education, and links to genuinely relevant educational resources, especially relevant articles from our Wellness Learning Center.
- Approximately 20% promotional or company-directed participation: WellPeps services, assessments, new programs, webinars, approved offers, and company content. Any promotional-minded engagement should be aware of the platform’s community culture.

The 80/20 principle is a planning guide, not permission to violate group rules or platform policies. In communities that prohibit promotion, the permitted promotional percentage is zero.

## 2.2 What Success Looks Like

- Useful answers that stand on their own even if WellPeps is never mentioned
- Trust and positive recognition over time
- Accurate explanations of the telehealth process
- Relevant traffic to educational resources
- Appropriate referrals to the WellPeps assessment process
- Fast and correct escalation of complaints, privacy issues, adverse events, emergencies, legal threats, or media inquiries

Likes and follower counts may be measured, but they are not the primary definition of success.

# 3. Roles, Accounts and Permission Levels

## 3.1 Trained WellPeps Community Team / Employees

Trained personnel may answer general questions, explain WellPeps processes, share approved educational information, link to approved resources, and participate in relevant communities when permitted. They may not diagnose, prescribe, recommend an individual treatment, interpret laboratory results, or create new medical claims.

## 3.2 Official WellPeps Accounts

Official accounts may speak in the company voice, answer process and service questions, share approved educational materials, respond to public comments, and direct people to appropriate WellPeps channels. They should remain educational and professional rather than sounding like a sales account.

## 3.3 Influencers and Brand Ambassadors

Influencers and Brand Ambassadors operate in a narrower lane. Their primary role is to share approved content and truthful personal experiences, disclose their relationship clearly, and direct medical or individualized questions to WellPeps or a licensed healthcare provider.

- Do not answer individualized medical questions.
- Do not recommend a medication or dosage.
- Do not create or improvise clinical claims.
- Do not imply that personal results are typical or guaranteed.
- Use required FTC sponsorship disclosures in addition to any WellPeps affiliation language.

## 3.4 Licensed Providers

Providers may be valuable educational participants, but public interaction can create additional clinical, licensing, privacy, and physician-patient relationship concerns. Provider participation should occur only under specifically approved clinical and compliance guidance.

## 3.5 Anonymous, Fake or Undisclosed Promotional Accounts

Prohibited. Representatives must not create or use accounts that conceal a WellPeps relationship in order to praise, recommend, defend, or steer conversations toward WellPeps.

# 4. Disclosure and Transparency

## 4.1 When Disclosure Is Required

Disclose the WellPeps relationship whenever discussing WellPeps, its services, its content, or a subject in a context where the affiliation could affect how the audience evaluates the statement.

## 4.2 Approved Affiliation Language

- “I work with WellPeps.”
- “I’m part of the WellPeps team.”
- “I’m a WellPeps Brand Ambassador.”
- “I partner with WellPeps.”

Sponsored creators must also use clear, conspicuous FTC-compliant sponsorship disclosures appropriate to the platform and content.

## 4.3 Disclosure Must Be Easy to Notice

Do not bury a disclosure in a profile, long hashtag string, collapsed text, or another location where a reasonable viewer may miss it. The relationship should be apparent in the communication itself when required.

# 5. Finding and Selecting Conversations

## 5.1 Appropriate Places to Engage

- Public Facebook groups where business participation is permitted
- Relevant Reddit communities where rules allow participation
- Comments on WellPeps-owned Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, YouTube, Facebook, or other approved channels
- Public posts where a WellPeps service, article, or process is directly relevant
- Wellness, healthy living, telehealth, weight management, hair restoration, sexual wellness, healthy aging, and related communities when participation complies with the community's rules

## 5.2 Conversation Selection Test

Before entering a third-party conversation, ask:

1. Can I add useful information independent of selling WellPeps?
2. Do the platform and community rules permit this type of participation?
3. Can I disclose my affiliation naturally and clearly?
4. Can I answer without individualized medical advice or an unapproved claim?
5. Would participation improve trust rather than look opportunistic?

If the answer to any of these is no, do not participate.

## 5.3 Conversations to Avoid

- Threads where commercial participation is prohibited
- Highly emotional disputes where WellPeps has no useful role
- Requests for diagnosis, dosage, medication changes, or individualized clinical judgment
- Conversations requiring access to confidential or patient information
- Threads where repeated WellPeps participation would reasonably look like spam
- Situations in which the representative has a personal conflict or cannot remain professional

# 6. Standard Engagement Workflow

1. Read the entire relevant post or thread before responding.
2. Check community and platform rules.
3. Determine whether you are participating as an employee/team member, official account, influencer/ambassador, or other authorized role.
4. Disclose affiliation when required.
5. Classify the question: general education, WellPeps process, service/pricing, individualized medical question, complaint, privacy issue, adverse event, emergency, legal/media/regulatory issue, or other.
6. Use only approved information and claims.
7. Answer the question directly and concisely.
8. Link only when the resource genuinely helps answer the question.
9. Do not continue a public exchange once personal health information or a matter requiring private support is involved.
10. Escalate when required.
11. Record the interaction when the tracking procedure applies.

# 7. What Representatives May Discuss

- General wellness and healthy lifestyle practices
- Nutrition, exercise, sleep, hydration, stress management and healthy habits
- Telehealth and virtual healthcare generally
- How the WellPeps assessment, provider review, and ongoing support process works
- General educational information about treatment categories using approved materials
- Publicly available WellPeps educational content
- WellPeps services, availability, pricing, and process using current approved company information
- The importance of evaluation by licensed healthcare providers

# 8. What Representatives Must Not Do

- Diagnose or suggest that a person's symptoms indicate a specific condition.
- Recommend a specific medication, treatment, dosage, schedule, or medication change to an individual.
- Interpret laboratory results.
- Tell someone to start, stop, increase, decrease, or substitute medication.
- Guarantee outcomes or promise weight loss, hair restoration, sexual-health improvement, wellness results, or other clinical results.
- State or imply that everyone qualifies.
- Make unapproved superiority, safety, efficacy, comparative, or FDA-related claims.
- Discourage someone from seeking care from another qualified healthcare professional.
- Request or disclose protected health information in a public setting.
- Confirm publicly that a person is a WellPeps patient.
- Discuss confidential company information.
- Argue, insult, retaliate, threaten, or engage in deceptive grassroots promotion.

# 9. Medical Questions: The Boundary

## 9.1 General Education Is Permitted

A representative may explain general concepts using approved information. Example: describing, at a high level, that different treatment categories have different mechanisms, eligibility considerations, risks, benefits, and provider-evaluation requirements.

## 9.2 Individualized Advice Is Not Permitted

Once the question becomes “What should I take?”, “Am I a good candidate?”, “What dose should I use?”, “What do these symptoms mean?”, or otherwise requires clinical judgment about the individual, stop short of an answer and refer the person to a licensed provider.

## 9.3 Standard Boundary Response

> Approved Pattern “That’s something a licensed healthcare provider would need to evaluate based on your individual medical history. I can provide general educational information about the topic, but I can’t recommend a treatment for you personally.”

# 10. Privacy, HIPAA and Moving Conversations Private

## 10.1 Never Request Publicly

- Medical records or laboratory reports
- Prescription details
- Dates of birth
- Insurance information
- Photographs intended for clinical evaluation
- Other protected or sensitive health information

## 10.2 If Someone Posts Personal Health Information

Do not repeat, quote, analyze, or amplify the information. Politely redirect the person away from the public thread and to an approved private/support channel where permitted.

## 10.3 Private Messages Are Not a Clinical Back Door

Moving a conversation to DM does not authorize the community representative to provide medical advice or collect information outside approved workflows. Private channels should be used for appropriate customer-service routing, account assistance, or directing the individual to the approved clinical/assessment process.

| Community post/question | I’m already a WellPeps patient. Here are my medications and lab results. What should I do? |
|---|---|
| Do not say | “Your labs look fine. Keep taking your medication.” |
| Approved approach | “For your privacy, please don’t post personal medical information here. I can help direct you to the appropriate WellPeps support/provider channel, but I can’t interpret your labs or advise you on treatment.” |
| Why | The post contains personal medical information and requires clinical judgment. |

# 11. Linking and Promotion Rules

## 11.1 Wellness Learning Center

Educational articles may be shared when they directly answer or add useful context to the discussion. The representative should briefly answer the question first when practical rather than dropping a link with no explanation.

## 11.2 Program or Assessment Links

A program or assessment link may be provided when the person asks how to get started, asks about WellPeps services, or the link is a natural next step after an appropriate explanation. Do not insert a sales link into unrelated discussions.

## 11.3 Avoid Repetitive Promotion

Do not post the same promotional language across multiple groups or threads in a way that resembles spam. Community participation should be contextual and individualized to the conversation while remaining within approved messaging.

# 12. Platform-Specific Operating Guidance

## 12.1 Facebook Groups

- Read and follow each group's rules before participating.
- If promotional participation requires administrator permission, obtain permission before posting.
- Participate consistently rather than appearing only when promoting WellPeps.
- Answer general questions and contribute educational information.
- Share WellPeps content only when genuinely relevant.
- Do not pressure members through unsolicited direct messages.

## 12.2 Reddit

- Always disclose affiliation when discussing WellPeps.
- Do not use anonymous promotional or astroturf accounts.
- Prioritize answers that are useful even without mentioning WellPeps.
- Respect subreddit rules and moderator decisions.
- Avoid repetitive links, promotional language, vote manipulation, coordinated praise, or defensive pile-ons.
- If a WellPeps mention is not necessary to answer the question, consider leaving it out.

## 12.3 Instagram

- Use approved messaging in posts, comments, stories, and reels.
- Use required sponsorship disclosures.
- Keep comment replies concise and educational.
- Redirect individualized medical questions to licensed providers/approved channels.
- Do not solicit personal health information in comments.

## 12.4 TikTok

- Avoid sensational, exaggerated, or guaranteed-result language.
- Use approved scripts for treatment-related content.
- Disclose sponsored relationships clearly.
- Do not turn comment questions into individualized treatment recommendations.
- Escalate adverse-event or safety-related comments.

## 12.5 LinkedIn

- Maintain a professional, informational tone.
- Use for company updates, educational articles, healthcare trends, partnerships, and thought leadership.
- Avoid patient-specific discussions.
- Do not make unapproved clinical or comparative claims.

## 12.6 YouTube and Other Public Comment Channels

Apply the same standards: disclose when appropriate, answer general questions, do not provide individualized advice, protect privacy, moderate according to platform policy, and escalate safety or complaint issues.

# 13. Complaints, Negative Comments and Misinformation

- Complaint Response Pattern
1. • Acknowledge the concern without admitting facts you cannot verify.
2. • Do not debate the person's experience.
3. • Do not request account or medical details publicly.
4. • Move appropriate support matters to an approved private channel.
5. • Escalate internally and document the interaction.

> Example “I’m sorry to hear about the concern. I’m part of the WellPeps team and would like to help get this to the right person. For your privacy, please don’t post medical or account details here. Please use [approved support channel], and we’ll route the matter appropriately.”

## 13.2 Correcting Misinformation

Correct misinformation only when you have approved, reliable information and the correction is likely to help the community. Be respectful. Correct the information, not the person. Do not turn the exchange into an argument.

## 13.3 Attacks and Hostile Users

Do not retaliate. One factual clarification may be appropriate. If the conversation becomes abusive, repetitive, threatening, or unproductive, stop engaging and use platform moderation/escalation procedures.

# 14. Adverse Events, Emergencies and Safety Signals

Any report of a serious or unexpected health problem associated with a treatment, medication, or WellPeps experience must be escalated promptly under the current WellPeps safety procedure. Community personnel should not investigate clinically or determine causation.

## 14.1 Medical Emergency

Do not provide clinical instructions beyond directing the individual to seek immediate emergency medical care. Follow the current escalation procedure.

## 14.2 Adverse Event

- Do not dismiss or minimize the report.
- Do not state that the medication caused or did not cause the event.
- Do not troubleshoot treatment publicly.
- Preserve the relevant post/link or approved documentation.
- Escalate immediately to the designated internal contact/process.

> Important The source documents require immediate escalation of adverse events and medical emergencies but do not identify the specific WellPeps department, person, contact method, or response-time SLA. Those operational fields should be completed before this manual is issued for live use.

# 15. Legal, Regulatory, Media and Privacy Escalations

Escalate immediately when a communication involves:

- Legal threats or attorney contact
- Regulatory agency contact
- Media inquiries
- Potential HIPAA/privacy breach
- Threats of violence or credible safety threats
- Requests for confidential company information
- Any situation a representative believes could create material legal, clinical, privacy, or reputational risk

Do not improvise a company response. Preserve the communication and route it through the designated internal process.

# 16. Comment Moderation

## 16.1 Generally Respond

- Good-faith questions
- Reasonable criticism
- Requests for process information
- Educational questions within the representative's permitted scope

## 16.2 Hide/Remove When Permitted by Policy

- Spam and scams
- Clearly abusive or harassing content
- Repeated disruptive content
- Content violating platform/community rules
- Public posting of sensitive personal information when moderation tools and policy permit appropriate protection

## 16.3 Do Not Remove Merely Because It Is Negative

Legitimate criticism should not be deleted solely because it is unfavorable. Respond appropriately, route support issues, and escalate when necessary.

# 17. Influencer and Brand Ambassador Operating Rules

## 17.1 What Influencers May Do

- Share approved WellPeps content and offers
- Describe truthful personal experiences, subject to approved testimonial guidance
- Explain at a high level that WellPeps provides a telehealth assessment/provider-review process
- Direct followers to WellPeps educational resources or approved landing pages
- Answer non-medical logistical questions when supplied with current approved information

## 17.2 What Influencers Should Route to WellPeps

- Medication-selection questions
- Eligibility questions requiring clinical judgment
- Side effects or adverse events
- Complaints and account issues
- Questions about interactions, contraindications, dosing, lab interpretation, or treatment changes
- Media, legal, regulatory, or privacy matters

## 17.3 Influencer Comment Sections

An influencer is responsible for appropriate conduct in their own content and should not improvise answers simply because a follower asks in the comments. Medical questions should be redirected using approved language.

## 17.4 Testimonials

Testimonials must be truthful and reflect actual experience. They must not guarantee results or create misleading impressions about typical outcomes. Sponsored relationships must be disclosed.

# 18. Response Writing Standard

## 18.1 Preferred Structure

1. Answer the general question first.
2. Disclose affiliation when required.
3. State the boundary if individualized medical judgment is involved.
4. Provide an approved educational resource when useful.
5. Offer an appropriate next step without pressure.

## 18.2 Tone

- Human, calm and conversational
- Knowledgeable but not clinical unless the speaker is an authorized clinician acting under separate guidance
- Respectful, including toward competitors and other healthcare professionals
- Concise; avoid jargon
- Never sarcastic, combative, defensive, or condescending

## 18.3 Do Not Sound Like a Script

Approved responses are guardrails, not a requirement to paste identical wording into every conversation. Representatives may adapt ordinary non-clinical phrasing to sound natural as long as the meaning, disclosures, claims, and compliance boundaries do not change.

# 19. Response Library: Common Scenarios

| Community post/question | How does WellPeps work? |
|---|---|
| Approved approach | “WellPeps uses an online telehealth process. You complete a health assessment, and a licensed healthcare provider reviews your information to determine whether treatment is medically appropriate. If treatment is prescribed, ongoing support is part of the process.” |
| Why | General process information is appropriate. Use the current approved company description. |

Community post/question

Does everyone qualify?

Do not say

“Yes, almost everyone can get it.”

Approved approach

“No. Eligibility depends on an individual evaluation. Only a licensed healthcare provider can determine whether treatment is medically appropriate.”

Why

Do not imply universal eligibility.

Community post/question

I’m 5'10”, 235 pounds and have high blood pressure. Would tirzepatide be better for me than semaglutide?

Do not say

“Tirzepatide would probably be better for you.”

Approved approach

“I work with WellPeps. That’s something a licensed healthcare provider would need to determine based on your health history and other factors. I can explain the general differences between semaglutide and tirzepatide, but I can’t recommend which treatment would be appropriate for you personally.”

Why

The user is asking for individualized treatment selection.

Community post/question

How much weight will I lose?

Do not say

“Most people lose at least 25 pounds.”

Approved approach

“Results vary by individual, and no specific amount of weight loss can be guaranteed. A licensed provider can discuss treatment considerations after evaluating your health history.”

Why

Avoid promises and unsupported typical-result claims.

Community post/question

Which dose should I start with?

Do not say

“Start low and increase after a month.”

Approved approach

“Dosing decisions need to be made by the prescribing healthcare provider. I can’t recommend a dose through social media.”

Why

Dosing is individualized clinical advice.

Community post/question

I’m nauseated after my injection. Should I skip the next dose?

Do not say

“Yes, skip it until you feel better.”

Approved approach

“I can’t advise you to change your medication or dosing. Please contact your prescribing provider through the appropriate care channel. If you believe you may be experiencing a medical emergency, seek immediate emergency medical care.”

Why

Medication changes and symptom assessment require clinical evaluation; the report may also require internal escalation.

Community post/question

Is semaglutide safe?

Do not say

“Yes, it’s very safe.”

Approved approach

“All medications can have potential risks and benefits. Whether a treatment is appropriate for a particular person requires evaluation by a licensed healthcare provider.”

Why

Avoid blanket safety assurances.

Community post/question

Is WellPeps cheaper than [competitor]?

Do not say

“Yes, and our care is better.”

Approved approach

“I work with WellPeps. I can explain our current pricing and what is included. I wouldn’t want to make a comparison unless we have current, verified information about the other service.”

Why

Comparative claims require approved, current support.

Community post/question

WellPeps ripped me off.

Do not say

“That’s not true. You must have misunderstood.”

Approved approach

“I’m part of the WellPeps team. I’m sorry to hear you have a concern. I’d like to help route this to the right person. For your privacy, please don’t post account or medical details here; please use our approved support channel.”

Why

Do not argue or litigate a complaint in public.

Community post/question

I had a terrible reaction after taking my medication.

Do not say

“That’s probably unrelated.”

Approved approach

“I’m sorry you’re dealing with that. I can’t assess symptoms or determine the cause here. Please contact your healthcare provider promptly, and if you believe this is a medical emergency, seek immediate emergency medical care.”

Why

Do not determine causation. Follow WellPeps adverse-event escalation procedures immediately.

Community post/question

Anyone know how online GLP-1 clinics work?

Do not say

“Use WellPeps—we’re the best.”

Approved approach

“I work with WellPeps. In general, telehealth clinics use licensed providers to review health information and determine whether treatment is appropriate. Regardless of the company, it’s important that treatment decisions are made by a licensed clinician.”

Why

The response provides value independent of promotion and clearly discloses affiliation.

Community post/question

Can you look at my lab report if I DM it to you?

Do not say

“Sure, send it over.”

Approved approach

“I can’t interpret laboratory results or collect them for clinical review through community messaging. A licensed provider should review your labs through the appropriate care channel.”

Why

DMs do not change the medical-advice or privacy boundary.

# 20. Content Approval and Use of Approved Materials

Unless specifically authorized otherwise, public content referencing WellPeps must be approved before publication. This includes videos, reels, TikToks, graphics, blog posts, educational posts, influencer content, and promotional materials.

- Do not modify approved medical claims in a way that changes their meaning.
- Do not create new clinical claims from memory or personal research.
- Use the current version of approved pricing, program descriptions, disclosures, and links.
- If a platform format requires shortening, preserve the required disclosure and substantive meaning.

# 21. Community Team Operating Routine

## 21.1 Start of Shift

1. Review internal alerts or updated approved messaging.
2. Check owned-channel comments and messages.
3. Review the escalation queue and unresolved items.
4. Review assigned communities and relevant conversations.
5. Confirm that any planned posts have required approval.

## 21.2 During Shift

1. Prioritize safety, privacy, complaints, and direct questions on owned channels.
2. Engage selectively in third-party communities.
3. Document meaningful interactions and referrals.
4. Escalate issues immediately when required.
5. Avoid over-posting in any single community.

## 21.3 End of Shift

1. Confirm escalations were handed off.
2. Record unresolved conversations requiring follow-up.
3. Log notable questions or recurring misinformation for content planning.
4. Flag outdated or missing approved responses/resources.

# 22. Documentation and Engagement Log

A centralized log should be maintained for material community activity. The source documents establish performance and escalation expectations but do not specify the tracking system. WellPeps should designate the system before launch.

## 22.1 Recommended Fields

- Date/time
- Platform and community
- Post/thread URL or identifier
- Representative/account
- Interaction category

- Disclosure used

## 22.2 What Not to Copy Into the Marketing Log

Do not unnecessarily reproduce medical details or other sensitive personal information. Record only what is needed to document the interaction and escalation.

# 25. Escalation Matrix

| Issue | Representative action | Escalation | Public response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual medical advice | Do not answer clinically | Provider/approved clinical channel | General boundary + provider referral |
| Adverse event | Do not assess causation; preserve interaction | Immediate safety/clinical escalation | Provider/emergency guidance as appropriate |
| Medical emergency | Do not troubleshoot | Immediate escalation | Seek immediate emergency medical care |
| Patient complaint | Acknowledge; move account details private | Customer support/management | Neutral acknowledgement |
| Privacy/HIPAA concern | Stop public discussion; preserve facts | Immediate privacy/compliance escalation | Minimal response; no confirmation of patient status |
| Legal threat | Do not debate | Immediate legal/management escalation | Acknowledge only if approved |
| Media inquiry | Do not provide interview/comment | PR/management | Refer to authorized spokesperson |
| Regulator contact | Do not respond substantively | Legal/compliance/management | Refer internally |
| Threat/safety concern | Do not escalate conflict | Management/platform/safety process | Minimal, safety-focused response |

Implementation Required Before we begin Community Engagement, WellPeps should replace generic escalation destinations above with named departments/roles, approved contact methods, business-hours and after-hours procedures, and required response times.

# 26. Supervisor Quality Assurance

- Review a sample of community interactions regularly.
- Check disclosure, accuracy, tone, privacy, claims, links, and escalation decisions.
- Monitor judgment, not just wording.
- Maintain a list of recurring questions requiring new approved responses.
- Immediately correct material misinformation or non-compliant content where feasible.
- Document significant coaching or corrective actions under the applicable personnel/contractor process.

# 27. Decision Tree: Can I Respond?

1. Is this a permitted community/channel and does participation comply with its rules? If no, stop.
2. Can I be transparent about my WellPeps relationship? If no, stop.
3. Is the question general education, WellPeps process, or other approved non-clinical information? If yes, respond using approved information.
4. Does it require individualized diagnosis, medication selection, dosing, lab interpretation, or treatment change? If yes, do not answer clinically; refer to a licensed provider.
5. Does it involve personal health information? Protect privacy and move only appropriate support routing to an approved private channel.
6. Does it involve an adverse event, emergency, legal threat, privacy concern, regulator, media inquiry, or serious complaint? Escalate.
7. Would the response still feel useful if the WellPeps promotional element were removed? If no, reconsider whether participation is appropriate.

# 28. Pre-Post Checklist

- □ I have read enough context to understand the conversation.
- □ The platform/community rules permit my participation.
- □ My affiliation is disclosed when required.
- □ My information comes from current approved materials.
- □ I am educating rather than diagnosing or prescribing.
- □ I have not promised or guaranteed an outcome.
- □ I have not made an unapproved comparative or medical claim.
- □ I have not requested or exposed personal health information.
- □ Any link I am sharing genuinely helps answer the question.
- □ I am not entering the conversation merely to promote WellPeps.
- □ I know whether this interaction requires escalation.
- □ I would be comfortable with this response being publicly attributed to WellPeps.

If any answer is “No” or “I’m not sure,” do not post until the issue has been reviewed.

# 29. Supervisor / Trainer Scenario Exercises

Use these exercises during onboarding. Trainees should identify: (1) whether to respond, (2) whether disclosure is required, (3) what may be said, (4) whether escalation is required, and (5) what should be documented.

- A Reddit user asks whether semaglutide or tirzepatide is better for someone with their medical conditions.
- A Facebook group member asks how telehealth weight-management programs generally work.
- An Instagram commenter says they were charged incorrectly by WellPeps and posts their full name and prescription.
- A TikTok viewer reports severe symptoms after a medication.
- A user asks an ambassador what dose they personally take and whether the user should take the same amount.
- A journalist messages a community representative asking for comment on compounded medications.
- A group member posts inaccurate information about WellPeps pricing.
- A moderator warns that a WellPeps representative is posting too many links.

# 30. Governance and Continuous Improvement

The manual should be reviewed at least annually and whenever there are material regulatory changes, platform-policy changes, new WellPeps services, changes to approved claims or pricing, privacy/compliance updates, or lessons learned from community activity.

Community personnel should be encouraged to submit recurring questions, unclear situations, missing scripts, and emerging platform issues to the WellPeps Marketing Governance Team or other designated owner.
