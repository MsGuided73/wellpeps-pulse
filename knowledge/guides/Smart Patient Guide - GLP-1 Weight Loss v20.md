## What Are GLP-1 Medications?

*UNDERSTAND THE TREATMENT*

GLP-1 stands for glucagon-like peptide-1, a hormone produced naturally by the body.

Medications that act on the GLP-1 pathway can affect appetite, digestion and blood-sugar regulation. Among other effects, GLP-1 receptor agonists can help people feel fuller after eating and may slow the rate at which food leaves the stomach. When clinically appropriate, these effects can support weight management.

Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist. Tirzepatide works somewhat differently because it acts on both GLP-1 and GIP receptors.

Medication does not make nutrition, physical activity, sleep and other healthy habits irrelevant. Weight-management medications are generally intended to accompany lifestyle changes rather than replace them.

## When Might a Customized Formulation Be Considered?

*PERSONALIZED TREATMENT OPTIONS*

FDA-approved medications are generally the first consideration for GLP-1 treatment. But medicine isn’t always one-size-fits-all.

In certain circumstances, a licensed healthcare provider may determine that an individual patient’s medical needs are better addressed by a customized compounded formulation or dosage that differs from a commercially available medication.

Depending on the patient’s individual needs and the options available through the dispensing pharmacy, a medical provider may prescribe compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide formulations that incorporate ingredients such as:

- **Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine):** An essential vitamin involved in metabolism, nervous-system function and the body’s use of nutrients.
- **Vitamin B12 (Cyanocobalamin):** An essential vitamin involved in red blood cell formation, nerve function and cellular energy metabolism.
- **L-Carnitine (Levocarnitine):** A naturally occurring substance involved in transporting fatty acids into cells where they can be used in energy production.
- **Glycine:** An amino acid used by the body in protein production and a variety of metabolic processes.
- **Niacinamide (Vitamin B3):** A form of vitamin B3 involved in cellular energy metabolism and normal cellular function.

## Injections, Tablets and What “FDA-Approved” Means

*KNOW THE DIFFERENCES*

The names on a program’s menu tell you less than you might think. Here is what actually separates the options.

- **SEMAGLUTIDE:** GLP-1 receptor agonist. FDA-approved brands: Wegovy (weight management), Ozempic (type 2 diabetes). Weekly injection; a tablet form also exists.
- **TIRZEPATIDE:** Acts on GIP and GLP-1 receptors. FDA-approved brands: Zepbound (weight management), Mounjaro (type 2 diabetes). Weekly injection.
- **INJECTION:** One weekly dose from a small pen. Most weight-management evidence comes from injectable forms.
- **ORAL TABLET:** A daily tablet for patients who prefer to avoid needles. Timing and food rules matter more.

“FDA-approved” means that specific product was reviewed by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for safety and effectiveness. Compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide is prepared by a licensed pharmacy for an individual patient and is not itself FDA-approved, so the pharmacy’s licensing and sourcing matter. Newer molecules such as retatrutide are investigational and not approved outside clinical trials.

## Side Effects Are Part of the Conversation

*KNOW WHAT TO WATCH FOR*

Every prescription medication has potential risks and side effects.

With GLP-1 therapies, commonly reported side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, abdominal discomfort and decreased appetite. Gastrointestinal symptoms may be more noticeable when treatment begins or following dose increases and may improve over time for some patients.

- **KNOW:** Understand common effects
- **WATCH:** Pay attention to persistent or worsening symptoms
- **CONTACT:** Seek appropriate clinical guidance when needed

Patients should follow the instructions provided with their medication and contact an appropriate healthcare professional about persistent, worsening or concerning symptoms. Potentially serious symptoms may require urgent or emergency medical evaluation.

Patients should not independently stop, restart or change prescribed medication without appropriate clinical guidance.
