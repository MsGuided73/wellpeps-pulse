*The Smart Patient’s Guide to Modern Healthcare: How telehealth, at-home testing and connected care are changing the patient experience*

## Healthcare Is Changing

*BEFORE YOU START*

For generations, healthcare largely revolved around the doctor’s office.

Make an appointment. Drive to the office. Sit in the waiting room. See the provider. Go somewhere else for testing. Go somewhere else for medication. Call again if you have a question.

Technology is changing that experience. Today, telehealth, online health assessments, patient portals, at-home testing, national laboratory networks and connected pharmacy services can make appropriate healthcare more accessible and convenient.

But convenience shouldn’t mean lower standards. The real opportunity isn’t simply moving healthcare online.

**It’s using technology to make good healthcare easier to access and easier to navigate.**

> **Smart Patient Insight**
>
> Modern healthcare should remove unnecessary friction and inconvenience—but not remove appropriate medical oversight.

## Healthcare Is Moving Beyond the Doctor’s Office

*THE NEW PATIENT EXPERIENCE*

Healthcare doesn’t always have to happen in one physical location.

Depending on the patient’s needs, parts of the healthcare experience can now take place from home:

- **HEALTH ASSESSMENTS:** Share health history, medications and other relevant information securely online.
- **MEDICAL REVIEW:** Licensed healthcare providers can review patient information remotely and determine appropriate next steps.
- **LABORATORY TESTING:** Testing may be completed through national laboratory networks or, for certain tests, through at-home collection.
- **PRESCRIPTION FULFILLMENT:** When treatment is prescribed, medication can often be shipped directly to the patient.
- **PATIENT SUPPORT:** Secure online portals and messaging can make it easier to ask questions and follow the treatment process.

The result isn’t healthcare without providers, pharmacies or laboratories.

**It’s a new way of connecting them around the patient.**

> **Smart Patient Insight**
>
> The biggest change in modern healthcare may not be what care you receive—but how you access it.

## What Is Telehealth, Really?

*UNDERSTAND TELEHEALTH*

Telehealth is sometimes described as simply “seeing a doctor on your computer.” It’s broader than that.

Telehealth uses technology to provide or support healthcare when the patient and healthcare provider aren’t in the same physical location. Depending on the type of care, that can include:

**Online health assessments**

**Video or telephone consultations**

- **Secure messaging**
- **Provider review of health information**
- **Review of laboratory results**
- **Prescription management**
- **Patient portals**
- **Ongoing communication and support**

Not every telehealth experience requires a video appointment, and not every medical issue can be handled remotely. The technology is simply a way to connect patients and healthcare professionals when remote care is appropriate.

> **Smart Patient Question**
>
> Am I receiving meaningful healthcare through technology—or simply being sold medication online?

## What Does Telehealth Do Well?

*KNOW ITS VALUE*

Telehealth can be particularly useful when appropriate care does not require the patient and healthcare provider to be in the same room.

Depending on the situation, telehealth can make it easier to:

- **SHARE HEALTH INFORMATION:** Complete assessments and provide relevant medical information before treatment.
- **ACCESS MEDICAL REVIEW:** Allow a licensed healthcare provider to evaluate whether a particular treatment may be appropriate.
- **REVIEW RESULTS:** Discuss laboratory or other health information remotely when appropriate.
- **MANAGE PRESCRIPTION CARE:** Receive instructions and communicate about prescribed treatment.
- **ASK QUESTIONS:** Use secure communication to obtain support without scheduling an office visit every time a question arises.

And for many patients, there are practical benefits as well:

**Convenience. Comfort. Privacy. Speed.**

> **Smart Patient Insight**
>
> Telehealth works best when its convenience and accessibility support appropriate care—not when they replace it.

## Good Telehealth Knows When You Need In-Person Care

*KNOW ITS LIMITS*

Telehealth can make many forms of healthcare more convenient. But it isn’t appropriate for everything.

Some medical concerns may require:

**A HANDS-ON PHYSICAL EXAMINATION**

- **IMAGING OR DIAGNOSTIC PROCEDURES**
- **IN-PERSON SPECIALTY CARE**
- **URGENT CARE**
- **EMERGENCY MEDICAL ATTENTION**

Laboratory testing doesn’t necessarily require abandoning telehealth. Depending on the situation, patients may be able to use local laboratory networks or appropriate at-home collection options while their broader care remains connected digitally.

**The important distinction is recognizing when remote care is appropriate—and when it isn’t.**

> **Smart Patient Insight**
>
> Good telehealth should know when not to be telehealth.

> **Smart Patient Question**

## The Right Testing, When It’s Appropriate

*LABS STILL MATTER*

Modern healthcare doesn’t mean testing everyone for everything.

Some treatments may require laboratory testing. Others may not. For certain types of care, laboratory results can help a healthcare provider:

*CONNECT THE PIECES*

## Not Every Online Healthcare Company Is the Same

*WATCH FOR THE RED FLAGS*

The growth of telehealth has given patients more choices. That makes it even more important to know what to look for.

- **Guaranteed prescriptions** — A prescription should follow appropriate medical review—not be promised before it occurs.
- **Miracle outcomes** — Be cautious of guaranteed weight loss, age reversal, hair regrowth, sexual performance or other treatment outcomes.
- **Little or no meaningful medical review** — Convenience shouldn’t eliminate appropriate provider involvement.
- **Unclear pharmacy fulfillment** — Once medication is prescribed and assigned for fulfillment, you should be able to identify the pharmacy handling your prescription.
- **Confusing pricing** — You should understand what treatment costs and what is included.
- **Hard-to-cancel programs** — Understand cancellation terms before enrolling.
- **No clear way to get support** — Know where to turn if you have treatment-related questions.
- **Claims that sound too good to be true** — Scientific-sounding language doesn’t automatically make a marketing claim scientifically proven.

> **Smart Patient Insight**
>
> Convenience is valuable. Transparency matters just as much.

## The Smart Patient’s Modern Healthcare Checklist

*USE THE CHECKLIST*

You don’t need to understand every piece of healthcare technology. You need to know enough to evaluate the care behind it.

- **01 Is a licensed healthcare provider involved?:** Who is responsible for determining whether treatment is appropriate?
- **02 Is my health information actually reviewed?:** Does the process consider my health history, medications and individual circumstances?
- **03 Is testing available when appropriate?:** If laboratory testing is relevant to my care, how is it handled?
- **04 Do I know who is fulfilling my prescription?:** Once my prescription is approved and assigned, can I see which pharmacy is fulfilling and shipping my medication?
- **05 Do I understand my prescription?:** Do I understand what medication was prescribed, how I should use it and what instructions I should follow?
- **06 What happens after treatment begins?:** Is there a clear way to ask questions or obtain support?
- **07 Is pricing clear?:** Do I understand the cost and what is included?
- **08 Can I cancel without the runaround?:** Are the cancellation terms straightforward and easy to understand?
- **09 Does the service know its limits?:** Will I be directed to in-person or urgent care when remote care isn’t appropriate?

**Evaluate the healthcare behind the technology—not just the convenience of the technology itself.**
